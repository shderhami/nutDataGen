## 1. What this ingredient is, and the serving-basis identity

Homemade pressure-cooked bone paste: **1000 g raw chicken bones + 250 mL water**, cooked in a closed pressure cooker and blended (Shahab, 2026-08-07). The bones are kitchen carcass bones, **stripped quite clean** of meat (Shahab's calibration answer, 2026-08-07), marrow necessarily retained (it is inside the bones).

A closed pot conserves mass, so the paste's composition equals the sum of its inputs. The DB portion (`portion_qty` = 125 g) therefore decomposes exactly:

> **125 g portion = 100 g raw bone + 25 g added water**

Every per-portion value in `ingredient_nutrients` equals the per-100 g-raw-bone value, plus 25 g on the Water row. Assumption: no venting loss (a small steam release at depressurization would concentrate the paste ~1–3%; covered by the Water row's min/max).

## 2. Why the original (AI-derived) profile was discarded

The entire row set carried `source='literature', comment='Source: AI'` — generated by the AI validation pipeline, not from any actual publication. It failed independent checks:

1. **Carbohydrate 15.93 g/portion (12.7%)** — bone contains no carbohydrate; the row's own AI justification ("bones are composed primarily of mineral and protein") contradicted its value.
2. **Internally inconsistent minerals** — Ca 7000 mg at hydroxyapatite stoichiometry implies ~18 g of bone mineral, against a stored ash of 12.8 g in the same row set.
3. **Incoherent amino-acid justifications** — different AA rows cited protein contents of 12–15%, 15–17%, 20–22%, and 30–35% for the same material; histidine sat at 5.1% of CP (no animal protein looks like that).
4. **Taurine 0.65 mg/portion** — ~40× below what the adhering-tissue evidence supports (§5.5).
5. **Fatty acids** — linoleic acid at 4.4% of fat, vs ~19.5% measured in US corn/soy-fed chicken depot fat.

Decision (Shahab): re-derive everything from literature; do not preserve the AI Ca/P as anchors; recipe 4 (the one user of this ingredient) will be reformulated regardless.

## 3. The derivation model

Three layers, each grounded in measured data, with every mixing fraction swept into the stored `min_value`/`max_value`.

### 3.1 Skeleton: proximates of 100 g raw clean-stripped bone

Anchored on published analyses of poultry **deboning bone residue** (machine-stripped bone — the cleanest measured analog of hand-stripped carcass bones) and the one direct raw-bone measurement (Cansu & Boran 2015, found during validation):

| Anchor (see §6 for full citations) | Material | Water | CP | Fat | Ash |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mast & Opiacha 1987 | MDM poultry bone residue, as-is | 60.0 | 18.9 | 7.7 | 11.7 |
| Wallace & Froning 1979 | MDM bone residue, DM basis | — | 45.5 %DM | 20.7 %DM | 35 %DM |
| Antipova et al. 2013 | poultry bone residue | ~45 (implied) | 25 | 18.9 | 11.1 |
| Cansu & Boran 2015 | raw broiler tibia **with marrow**, as-is | 57.5 | 18.1 (N×6.25) | 9.5 | 14.7 |
| Kakimov et al. 2021 | meat-and-bone paste (meaty lower bound) | 65.6 | 18.3 | 11.8 | 4.35 |

**Adopted (per 100 g raw bone): water 61, CP 18, fat 8.5, ash 12.5, carbohydrate 0** — sums to 100.0 exactly. Notes:
- CP stays ~18 even for clean bones because bone-matrix collagen and unstrippable connective tissue *are* protein — the machine-stripped anchors prove it (18.9% / 45.5 %DM).
- Ash 12.5 sits at the clean end of the residue anchors; Cansu's 14.7 is dense tibia only — carcass mixes (rib/spine/keel cartilage, young under-mineralized broiler bone) sit lower. DM ash = 12.5/39 = 32% (Cansu: 34.6%; Wallace & Froning: 35%).
- Energy = FEDIAF modified Atwater: 3.5×CP + 8.5×fat + 3.5×NFE = 3.5×18 + 8.5×8.5 + 0 = **135.3 kcal/portion**.

### 3.2 Mineral chemistry: Ca, P, and the ash-borne trace elements

Bone ash is essentially bone mineral (carbonated hydroxyapatite), whose element fractions are crystal-chemistry properties — stable across individuals and diets:

- **Ca = 0.38 × ash** = 0.38 × 12.5 g = **4750 mg**. The 0.38 is triple-confirmed: Suchý et al. 2009 measured Ca/ash 0.372–0.386 across 120 broilers; Kakimov 2021's paste gives 1654/4350 = 0.380; NIST SRM 1400 bone ash certifies Ca 38.18%.
- **P = Ca/2.16 + ~50 mg soft-tissue P** = 4750/2.16 + 50 ≈ **2250 mg** (2.16 = hydroxyapatite Ca:P mass ratio). Resulting Ca:P = 2.11, centrally inside the measured broiler range 1.80–2.21 (§7).
- **Ash-borne traces** × 12.5 g ash: Mg 0.5–0.7% of ash (NIST certified 0.684%) → 75 mg; Na ~0.8% → 100 mg; Zn ~200 ppm (NIST certified 181–215 ppm) → 2.5 mg; K 186–600 ppm → only 3–9 mg (bone K is trivial; the K row is soft-tissue-driven); Cl ~0.13% → 16 mg.

### 3.3 Two-component profiles and the soft-tissue scale

The 100 g of raw bone is modeled as bone tissue (≈ 12.5 g mineral / 0.40 ≈ 31 g fresh) plus ≈ 70 g of everything else — cartilage, connective tissue, marrow, unstrippable scraps, and their water. Three consequences:

**Amino acids — CP 18 g = 70% collagen + 30% muscle-type protein** (range 60–80% collagen).
`AA_i = 18 × (0.70 × gelatin_i/85.6 + 0.30 × meat_i/21.39)`
where gelatin = USDA SR28 item 19177 (gelatin *is* hydrolyzed type-I collagen — Trp 0, Cys 0, Arg 7.7% of CP) and meat = SR28 05011 (chicken broiler meat, raw). The 70% is constrained twice: (a) floor — bone DM is ~25% collagen against ~65% mineral, so 12.5 g mineral alone drags in ≈ 4.8 g collagen before counting any connective tissue; (b) external fit — the 70/30 blend reproduces **meat-and-bone meal** feed-table lysine (~5.4% of CP) almost exactly, just as 50/50 reproduces the meatier poultry by-product meal. Muscle-equivalent tissue implied: 0.30 × 18 / 0.214 ≈ **25 g** — this number propagates to taurine and the vitamins.

**Fatty acids — fat 8.5 g = 90% depot/marrow + 10% intramuscular** (range 80–100% depot).
`FA_i = 8.5 × (0.90 × fat_i/99.8 + 0.10 × meatfat_i/3.08)`
where depot = SR28 04542 (chicken fat) and intramuscular = SR28 05011's own FA panel. Poultry marrow fat is storage triglyceride with a depot-like profile (Moerck & Ball 1973; Trindade 2004: marrow FA ≈ breast/thigh/skin). The 10% intramuscular share is bounded by the 25 g muscle-equivalent × 2–3% intramuscular fat ≈ 0.5–0.8 g; it matters only for the long-chain PUFAs (ARA/EPA/DHA/DPA from membrane phospholipids).

**Micronutrients — muscle-borne nutrients at 0.35 × SR28 05011 per-100 g values** (25 g muscle-equivalent plus partial cellularity of marrow/cartilage), plus bone-ash terms (§3.2), plus fat-borne terms (vitamins D/E and choline via SR28 04542: choline 122.4 mg/100 g fat from phospholipids). Iron is marrow-driven, not modeled from meat: anchored between Kakimov's measured 3.83 mg/100 g and the bone-ash-implied ~9 (NIST Fe 660 ppm), with MDM-vs-hand-deboned data (23.1 vs 4.2 mg/kg) confirming marrow dominates.

**Taurine — 25 mg (12–45).** Bone matrix (mineral + collagen) contains no taurine; it is an intracellular free amino acid of muscle and blood cells. Basis: 25 g muscle-equivalent × ~1.1 g/kg (dark-leaning back/neck muscle, from Spitze 2003: dark meat ~1690, light 180, necks+backs composite 584 mg/kg wet) + a marrow-cell contribution. The closed pressure cooker retains all liquor, so Spitze's cooking losses (leaching into discarded water) do not apply.

## 5. Worked examples

1. **Calcium:** ash 12.5 g × 0.38 (Ca fraction of bone ash; Suchý 0.372–0.386, NIST 0.3818, Kakimov 0.380) = 4.75 g → **4750 mg**.
2. **Lysine:** 18 g CP × (0.70 × 3.460/85.6 + 0.30 × 1.818/21.39) = 18 × (0.02830 + 0.02550) = **0.968 g**. Check: 0.968/18 = 5.4% of CP = meat-and-bone meal's measured feed-table value.
3. **Linoleic acid:** 8.5 g fat × (0.90 × 19.5/99.8 + 0.10 × 0.55/3.08) = 8.5 × (0.1759 + 0.0179) = **1.647 g** (19.4% of fat; USDA marrow-containing MDPM measures 20.6%).
4. **Zinc:** ash-borne 12.5 g × 200 ppm = 2.5 mg + soft tissue 0.35 × 1.54 = 0.54 mg → **3.0 mg**.
5. **Taurine:** 25 g muscle-equivalent × ~1.1 mg/g ≈ 27 mg, rounded to **25 mg** with the marrow contribution inside the 12–45 range. Sanity: ≈ 420 mg/kg on a DM basis, matching meat-and-bone meal's 405 mg/kg-DM midpoint and correctly below Spitze's meatier anchors (necks+backs 584, MDM meat 457 mg/kg wet).

## 8. Confidence assessment & how to improve it

- **High:** the 100 g bone + 25 g water accounting; carb = 0; Ca/ash, P/ash, Ca:P (measured chemistry); proximate closure (rows sum to exactly 125.000 g).
- **Medium:** the proximate centrals (ash especially — the single biggest lever; everything mineral scales with it); AA/FA profiles (measured component profiles, modeled mixing fractions, swept into min/max).
- **Lower:** muscle-borne vitamins (0.35 scale is modeled, not measured); vitamin D (inherently feed-dependent); **taurine** (no direct datum exists in the literature at all).

**Upgrade path:** (1) oven dry-matter test on the actual paste (~105 °C to constant weight) — nails water%, the denominator of all DM math, for free; (2) a feed-lab proximate + Ca/P panel (~$40–60) replaces every medium-confidence number with a measurement; (3) only a lab amino-acid panel (taurine included) would settle taurine — no kitchen test can.
